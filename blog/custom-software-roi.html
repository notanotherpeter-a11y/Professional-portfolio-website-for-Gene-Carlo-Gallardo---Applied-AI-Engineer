The Real ROI of Custom Software for Small Businesses

August 2026 · 4 min read · Gene Carlo Gallardo

Every SMB owner I've spoken to has the same experience with off-the-shelf software. It starts promising. Then you hit the wall — the feature you actually need is on the premium tier, the interface assumes a workflow that doesn't match yours, and customer support sends you a help article written for a business nothing like yours.

So you subscribe to three tools instead of one. You export from one, import into another, and spend your Friday afternoon reconciling a spreadsheet that bridges both. This is not efficiency. This is expensive workaround infrastructure.

What Off-the-Shelf Actually Costs

Let's be specific. A typical SMB running on off-the-shelf tools might pay:

  • Inventory: $50–200/mo (Cin7, DEAR, Lightspeed)
  • POS: $60–150/mo (Square, Shopify POS)
  • Payroll: $30–100/mo (Deputy, KeyPay)
  • Reporting: $30–80/mo (Xero, QuickBooks)
  • CRM/Orders: $50–150/mo (HubSpot, Monday)

That's $220–680/mo, minimum, for tools that weren't designed to talk to each other. Add integrations via Zapier or Make and you're paying for plumbing on top of the pipes.

The average SMB I work with is spending $300–500/month on software and still doing significant work manually to fill the gaps.

The Custom Software Argument

Here's what a custom system built for your specific business gives you:

  • One login — everything in one place, designed around your actual workflow
  • No per-seat pricing — add 10 staff for the same monthly cost
  • No bloat — only the modules you actually use, built the way you actually use them
  • Real ownership — the system can be modified, extended, or migrated. You're not locked in.

What It Actually Costs

For the businesses I build for, a custom ERP typically runs:

  • Setup: $5,000–$15,000 AUD depending on complexity
  • Monthly: $200–500/mo for hosting, maintenance, and support

Break-even against off-the-shelf usually happens within 12–18 months. After that, you're paying significantly less and getting a system that's actually built for you.

For Philippine businesses, the numbers are more compelling. Setup costs of ₱80,000–₱150,000 and monthly fees of ₱8,000–₱15,000 against off-the-shelf tools that often don't support Philippine payment rails, tax structures, or even the right currency formats.

When Custom Software Doesn't Make Sense

I'm not going to pretend custom software is always the answer. It's not right if:

  • You're pre-revenue or in the first 6 months of operation
  • Your processes change month-to-month and haven't stabilised yet
  • You need a standard tool that an accountant or external auditor expects (like Xero for bookkeeping)

In those cases, start with off-the-shelf, learn what you actually need, and then build when you know the shape of the problem.

The Right Question to Ask

Don't ask "can we afford custom software?" Ask "how much is our current workaround costing us?" Include subscription fees, the time staff spend on manual reconciliation, the errors that come from data not being in one place, and the decisions you're making blind because you don't have real-time visibility into your business.

Most of the time, the answer makes the decision obvious.
